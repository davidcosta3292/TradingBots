The plan

Three stages. One robot.
No shortcuts.

Nothing here changes how we trade. The robot follows the rules we already follow. It just never gets tired, never hesitates, and writes down every trade. Real money only comes after it has proven itself on a demo account.

01Build
Turn the strategy we already use into exact rules, code it into a robot, and backtest it on years of data.
Move on when
the backtest trades the way we do.
02Demo
The robot trades a demo account from our own PC (fake money, real prices) while we watch and control it from the office.
Move on when
weeks of demo match the backtest.
03Live
Same robot, same code, real account. Smallest size first; it earns every step up.
Scale up when
live keeps matching demo.

Stage by stage

What happens, and when we move on.

01
Build
the robot
Our strategy becomes code.
What happens
  • Write the strategy on one page: entry, stop, exit, size, trading hours, and the days we skip (news).
  • Code it with safety built in: a stop on every trade, max size, a daily loss limit, flat before the session ends, and a pause switch.
  • Backtest it on several years of data.
What we need
  • The platform: MetaTrader 5 or NinjaTrader 8
  • Our rules, written down
  • Historical data (comes with the platform)
We move on when
  • The backtest trades like we do (win rate, average win and loss, number of trades) in good and bad periods.
  • We both understand every rule.
02
Demo
account
Fake money, real market.
What happens
  • The robot runs on our own PC, set up to stay on. For the last 1–2 weeks it moves to a VPS, so the move happens on fake money.
  • A small reporter sends every trade, open position and a heartbeat (every 30 s) to our database.
  • We watch it in the office, get phone alerts, and compare demo with the backtest weekly.
What we need
  • A demo account (free)
  • Our PC: no sleep, no surprise restarts
  • A VPS for the final weeks ($12–30 a month)
We move on when
  •   weeks of demo (we pick 4–8) track the backtest.
  • Every button has been tested on demo.
  • The final weeks on the VPS ran without problems.
  • Kill rules for live are written down (page 7).
03
Live
account
Small first, then scale.
What happens
  • Same robot, same code. Only the account changes.
  • Start at the smallest size. The office, alerts and AI reports stay exactly the same.
  • A 30-minute weekly review comparing live, demo and backtest.
What we need
  • A live account: our own capital, or a prop firm that allows robots (page 6)
  • The kill rules
Scale up / switch off
  • Scale up when live matches demo for an agreed period.
  • Switch off when any kill rule triggers. No debating it in the moment.
The first thing stage 01 tells us: if a rule can't be written down, a robot can't trade it. Writing the one-page rules shows us which parts of our trading are really rules, and which are gut feel.

How it fits together

Five parts.
Only one of them trades.

Every part talks to one shared database, never directly to each other. That keeps the robot simple, and makes everything around it safe to build and change.

orders: only the robot information commands: Start, Pause, Done, Close (option B, decided)

Robot

Runs our strategy on MetaTrader 5 or NinjaTrader 8. The only part that can open or close a trade.

Reporter

A tiny add-on next to the robot. Sends trades, positions, balance and a heartbeat.

Database

Keeps every trade and snapshot and pushes live updates. Supabase, free to start.

3D office

Built on the open-source Claw3D project, plus P&L panels and phone alerts.

AI helpers

Claude agents: daily reports, risk warnings, and answers to “why did we lose today?”

1
Only the robot trades.Everything else reads. The most it can do is ask the robot to pause.
2
Everything has a heartbeat.If a part goes quiet for 2 minutes, we get an alert.
3
Passwords stay put.Trading logins never leave the trading computer.
Why the AI doesn't trade: in Alpha Arena (Oct–Nov 2025), AI models each traded $10,000 of real money, and most lost. The organiser's verdict: “LLMs can't really make money by themselves.” So our rules do the trading. The AI explains and warns.

Option B · our choice

Watch + command

The office is a control room.

Everything in A, plus four buttons on every robot:

Startmay take new trades
Pausefinish open trades, no new ones
Done for todayoff until the next session
Close everythingflatten now · double confirm
Effort MediumRisk Low, if built right
How a command travels e.g. pausing Robot 01
1
We press Pauseon Robot 01, in the office
2
Database saves itwho pressed it, what, and when
3
Robot checks and obeysevery few seconds, before any new trade
4
Robot confirmsthe office shows it as paused
If the office or the internet goes down, the robot simply keeps following its own built-in rules.
Guardrails for option B
1Buttons can only start, pause, stop or close. They can never open a trade or raise a limit.
2The robot's own limits always win over any button.
3Every press needs a login and a confirmation, and is logged with who and when.
4Check the venue's rules. At Topstep, a remote “close everything” may count as sending an order.
How we
build it

The buttons come first. The command loop is the first thing we build, on a test robot, before the strategy exists. Every button then gets weeks of testing on demo, so by the time real money is involved, we already trust them.

Where we trade

Where the robot trades
decides what's allowed.

On our own capital, brokers generally allow robots. Prop firms each set their own rules, and some ban robots completely. We checked each firm's own rules page on 23 Sep 2026.

WhereRobotsOn a VPS?What to know
Our own capitalMetaTrader 5 · e.g. forex, goldAllowedYesFull control of the robot and the account. Check the broker's terms for any banned strategies. It's our own money at risk.
Lucid Tradingfutures · NinjaTrader, TradovateAllowedNot statedRobots and trade copiers are allowed. High-frequency trading and micro-scalping are banned. Accounts left untraded for 30 days are deleted.
MyFundedFuturesfuturesAllowedNot statedOur own automation is allowed. No high-frequency trading, and no copying other traders.
Topstepfutures · TopstepXAPI onlyNo. Orders must come from our own computer.Robots go through their API: $29 a month, $14.50 with their code. A server may watch and record, but it can't send orders.
Take Profit TraderfuturesBannedn/aTheir rule #1: “No Trading Bots or Algos.”
Apex Trader FundingfuturesBannedn/a“No Automation or Algorithm Usage allowed.” Breaking it risks account closure and lost payouts.

What this means for us

On our own capital with MetaTrader 5, the plan works exactly as written. If we go the prop-firm route for futures, we pick a firm that allows robots (Lucid, MyFundedFutures, or Topstep's API), and never run the robot at Apex or Take Profit Trader.

Before paying for anything

Rules change often, and review sites regularly contradict the official pages. Before we buy an evaluation, we re-read that firm's own rules page, and keep a screenshot with the date.
